## Temperature Relevance

- **Mechanism:** Thermal stress at high temperatures; spat mortality during heat events
- **Degree-day relevance:** **Low to moderate** — less phenology-driven than mobile taxa
- **Known risk:** Temperature interacts with salinity for disease dynamics (Dermo)

## Salinity Relevance

- **Mechanism:** Primary acute mortality driver — freshening events from storm runoff cause mass kills
- **Osmotic stress pathway:** Strongest, most measurable signal for oysters in NC (reviewer note: strongest H3 pathway)
- **Salinity-disease interaction:** High salinity + high temperature enables *Perkinsus marinus* (Dermo) — treating high salinity as unconditionally "healthy" is incorrect
- **Known risk:** Disease confounding is a known limitation; deferred unless clean dataset found

## Dissolved Oxygen Relevance

- **Mechanism:** Sessile; cannot escape hypoxic water. Prolonged low DO causes mortality
- **Compression role:** Oysters experience compression as direct exposure, not spatial redistribution
- **Known risk:** Reef micro-habitat may buffer or amplify exposure vs. surrounding water column

## Turbidity / Light Relevance

- **Direct relevance:** Moderate — sedimentation/burial is a real threat but poorly measured (B2 deferred)
- **Proxy role:** Turbidity from runoff as indirect indicator of burial risk

## Habitat Dependency

- **Benthic:** Yes — obligately sessile on reef substrate
- **Substrate:** Hard substrate required (shell, rock, artificial reef)
- **Depth:** Intertidal to shallow subtidal
- **Spatial fixity:** Cannot relocate; exposure is total

## Degree-Day Relevance

- **Expected value:** Low — less phenological control than mobile crustaceans
- **B1 test:** Degree-days expected to add less for oyster than for blue crab or finfish
